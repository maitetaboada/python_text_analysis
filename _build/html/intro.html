Python Text Analysis#

Course description#

The course introduces basic concepts and tools for text analysis using the python programming language. It will address data capture and manipulation, data cleaning and preprocessing, and text analysis for linguistics and other social sciences.

Course objectives#

At the end of the course, students will have learnt the basic aspects of python programming. They will understand how to process language data for various analyses.

More specifically, students will:

Learn core concepts of programming (variables, functions, objects). Learn to install and use basic packages for text analysis (NLTK, spacy). Be able to collect and store a dataset using existing python packages. Clean and normalize language data. Perform natural language processing analysis on language data. Classes take place Wednesdays 1:30 - 2:20 pm in RCB 8100. I don’t have regular office hours, but I am on campus and in my office most days, so feel free to drop by my office in RCB 8109. You can also make an appointment for an in-person/zoom meeting.

Canvas will be your friend. I’ll post announcements, assignments, and all course materials there. I also expect you to communicate with me through Canvas rather than through email directly.

Teaching Assistant: Yifang Yuan (yifang_yuan_2@sfu.ca). Yifang will be your first point of contact for troubleshooting assignments and catching up on material.

Office hours#

Yifang will hold office hours:

Thursdays, 2:30 - 3:30, in RCB 9205 and on zoom Links to an external site. Additionally, there will be office hours the first couple of weeks to help you set up (see below, Equipment):

Tuesday, September 9, 2 pm - 4pm, in RCB 9205 and on zoom Links to an external site. Tuesday, September 16, 2pm - 4pm, in RCB 9205 and on zoom Links to an external site.

Equipment#

You will need a computer where you can install software (python and other packages). Please speak to me or to Yifang at the beginning of the term if you need any help with the technical stuff. You are supposed to have everything installed and ready to go by the end of week 2.

Course expectations#

This is a collaborative course, where we will learn from each other by coming to class prepared to discuss material and to work on labs. Part of the work will involve reading ahead of time and completing tasks at home that will allow us to troubleshoot in class. The course is not hard and it does not require a ton of work; it just needs attention, making sure you know what you are supposed to be doing every week.

Academic dishonesty#

Academic dishonesty in all forms violates the basic principles of integrity and thus impedes learning. Academic dishonesty includes: cheating, fabrication, fraud, facilitating academic dishonesty, collusion, and plagiarism.

Academic integrity is important to me, and I know it is to most students. Cheating is never worth the risk to your personal and professional reputation. I expect students to be truthful and honest in their work. This means that any work you submit for individual evaluation should be yours alone and that you recognize the work of others if you use their ideas, thoughts, or material. The assignments have been designed to ensure that you learn the material. I ensure that all students are graded fairly and will take action against students who violate the academic dishonesty policy. Students who engage in any acts of falsification, misrepresentation, or deception will face penalties for violating SFU policy, which can include failure in the assignment and/or a FD-Failed for Discipline grade on your transcript.

About ChatGPT and similar generative “AI” help: in this class, you’ll actually be learning about how such AI/NLP tools work. So I expect you to be a judicious user of any tools out there. If you do use them, you have to say so in your assignment and write 1-2 sentences critically reflecting on what it produced and why you think you got the output you did.

For more information on academic honesty and student conduct, please visit the following websites:

https://www.sfu.ca/students/calendar/2025/spring/fees-and-regulations/student-contract/academic-honesty.html http://www.sfu.ca/policies/gazette/student.html https://www.sfu.ca/students/academicintegrity/UsingGenerativeAI.html

Academic concessions#

It is possible that some of us will be sick or need to miss class during the semester. For a short absence (1-2 classes), you only have to:

Send me a message on Canvas telling me that you need to miss class/assignments. Make sure you catch up with any material you missed. This includes asking for extensions if you need them. For longer absences, please check SFU’s policies on academic concessions. And remember, you first deal with your health or personal emergency and then we’ll all help you figure out how to deal with the course requirements. Send me a message or ask for a meeting when you are able.

Please follow SFU’s communicable disease guidelines. The most important guideline is: don’t come to campus if you are sick.

Please note that students requiring accommodations as a result of a disability must contact the Center for Accessible Learning (778-782-3112 or caladmin@sfu.ca). All student requests for accommodation for religious practices must be made in writing by the end of the first week of classes or no later than one week after a student adds a course.

Mental health and wellness#

Your well-being is important. One of my goals for the semester is to provide a learning environment that supports and enhances your well-being, while creating an engaging learning experience. I welcome your feedback and input as we work together towards that goal. All SFU graduate, undergraduate and Continuing Studies (non-credit) students can access the SFU Health and Counselling Centre. Immediate support or counselling is also available 24/7 by phone (1-844-451-9700) or by chat using the MySSP app: https://www.sfu.ca/students/health/support-resources/my-ssp.html

Information on copyright#

Course materials such as PowerPoint slides, lecture notes, the lecture itself, assignments and exams are all protected by copyright. You are NOT allowed to reproduce them, mail them or upload them to any site for a purpose other than your own study. Selling course materials is a violation of copyright law, and a cheapening of your tuition dollars.

Components of the course#

The course consists of one hour of class time every week, during which we will mostly work on labs, practice exercises and group work.

Attendance is mandatory to pass the course, unless there are extenuating circumstances. You are also expected to be actively engaged in class discussions and activities. You can have up to 2 unexcused absences in the semester. Weekly reflections need to be completed every week. This is a short discussion or reflection on what you learned during the week. Labs are completed every week and graded on completion, showing a good effort. Assignments expand on the labs and are an opportunity to analyze data the students are interested in. They can be completed in groups of maximum 2 students. All the things you have to submit (reflections, labs, assignments) have a soft deadline (Friday, 11:59 pm) and a hard deadline (Sunday, 11:59 pm). You are supposed to submit them by Friday, especially if you need our help, as we cannot answer questions over the weekend. You can, however, submit on Sunday without a late penalty. We just won’t be able to provide any answers if you get stuck on the weekend. Anything submitted after Sunday 11:59 receives a grade of 0.

Course grade#

Note this course is a pass/fail course. The final grade will be calculated on a competency basis. If all components are completed with a satisfactory performance, the student will receive a P. Otherwise, the grade is F. Competency in this course is defined as at least 70% of the components completed satisfactorily.
